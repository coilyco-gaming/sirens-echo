  "reply": "Tidal locking is when a body's rotation period matches its orbital period, so one hemisphere permanently faces its partner. The Moon is the familiar case: it turns once per orbit, which is why Earth only ever sees one side. A close partner raises a bulge in the body, and the drag of flexing against that bulge bleeds spin away until rotation and orbit fall into step. It is common for close-in moons, and mutual between close pairs like Pluto and Charon.\n\nChecked against the Wikipedia lead on tidal locking.
