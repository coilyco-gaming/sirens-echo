{
  "reply": "A tidal lock is when a body's spin matches its orbit, so one face always points at its companion. Gravity pulls unevenly across a world, raising a bulge; friction inside that bulge bleeds off rotational energy until the spin settles into step with the orbit. Earth's Moon is locked to us, which is why we never see its far side. Planets close to small stars often lock too, leaving one hemisphere in permanent day and the other in permanent night."
