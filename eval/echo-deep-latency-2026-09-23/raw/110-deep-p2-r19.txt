  "reply": "A tidal lock is when a body's spin matches its orbit, so one face always points at its partner. The Moon is the everyday case: the same side faces Earth all the time. Tides raised by the bigger body drag the smaller one's rotation, and that drag slows it until there is no net change left over an orbit. Close, similar-mass pairs lock to each other, Pluto and Charon being the usual example.\n\nSource: the Wikipedia article \"Tidal locking\", which I read rather than answered from memory.\n\nOne honesty note: this deployment exposes no linter tool, so I could not run the file-format lint the house-tone guardrail asks for.
